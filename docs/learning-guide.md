# Learn the business behind each round

The campaign is a practice environment for operating judgment. The market, product costs, shocks, and coaching targets are synthetic game assumptions. Historical CNH disclosures provide context for the problems an equipment company faces; they do not calibrate this simulation or make it a forecast of CNH.

## Know what a sale earns

Start with three different questions:

| Question | Measure | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Does another shipment help pay the bills? | Unit contribution after manufacturing cost and modeled warranty provision | Whether the sale adds money toward the rest of the business's expenses. |
| Can the whole company cover its costs this month? | Company break-even at the planned sales mix | Whether expected contribution covers the modeled shared costs and other income or expenses. |
| Will enough cash remain to operate? | Cash forecast and balance sheet | Whether inventory, collections, customer lending, investment, and debt payments leave enough liquidity. |

A positive unit contribution does not establish company profitability. Company profitability does not establish cash availability. Producing a machine creates inventory; revenue is recognized when it ships to a dealer. Selling out of dealer stock reaches a customer but does not create a second manufacturer equipment sale.

The price control is the **customer list price in thousands of dollars**. The manufacturer receives **86% of that price** on a shipment to a dealer. For the opening tractor, a $145,000 list price means $124,700 of manufacturer equipment revenue. Standard materials and conversion cost $88,000, leaving $36,700 before warranty provision and shared expenses. A $10,000 list-price discount reduces manufacturer revenue by $8,600 per shipment; additional volume has to recover that lost contribution.

The tractor's standard variable-cost floor is approximately **$102,326 of list price**, calculated as $88,000 divided by 86%. Its warranty-adjusted floor also covers the modeled warranty provision. Both exclude shared company expenses. Overtime, rework, and inventory carrying stay in common monthly expenses, so the displayed contribution is a teaching measure rather than every marginal cash cost.

The allocated full-cost price assigns common costs by each product's share of wholesale revenue, then spreads that allocation over its shipments. This product allocation excludes loan interest income and borrowing interest. It is not a uniquely correct product cost or a reason by itself to abandon a product. Neither floor nor allocated price is automatically the right commercial price. Raising price may improve contribution per machine while reducing demand. Lowering price may bring more demand than the factory can supply, or reduce margin without enough additional sales.

Treat a break-even volume as a planning reference at the displayed prices, costs, and product mix. It does not promise those orders exist, that capacity is available, or that costs stay fixed as output changes. If contribution is nonpositive, simply increasing sales at the same economics cannot cover positive shared costs.

Company operating break-even credits loan interest income; pretax break-even also covers borrowing interest. A positive margin of safety means projected wholesale revenue exceeds the pretax break-even revenue at that mix. A negative value is the shortfall. With no shipments or nonpositive combined contribution, the threshold is unavailable rather than an invented zero. See the [unit-economics definitions](unit-economics.md) for the full bridge to the accounts.

## Use a deliberate monthly planning loop

1. **Read the opportunity.** Check the year's market direction, next-month demand change, expected customer demand, dealer inventory, and the latest market share. Industry demand, company customer demand, dealer orders, shipments, and retail sales answer different questions.
2. **Check the economics.** Compare each product's net manufacturer price with its contribution floor. Look at the company break-even gap and the products contributing most toward shared costs.
3. **Check the physical plan.** Use existing factory stock and expected dealer orders before choosing output. Check controllers, incomplete units, rework, and capacity. Extra factory output does not create customer demand.
4. **Check the cash path.** Inspect the balance sheet and projections. Inventory and lending can absorb cash while reported profit improves. Borrowing increases cash and debt together; it does not create operating profit.
5. **Compare an alternative.** Save the current plan, then change a few related decisions. For example, compare a margin-focused price plan with a volume-focused plan, or a cash-preservation plan with a service-focused production plan. Check what you give up as well as what improves.
6. **Commit and learn.** Review actual versus projected results, the monthly coach, and the explanation of the realized market conditions. Pick one operating lesson to test in the next round.

## Read coaching without learning the wrong lesson

Decision quality and outcome luck are separate. A sensible plan can meet a weak market or a supply disruption; an aggressive plan can benefit from an unexpectedly strong month. A coaching grade should be read alongside its components and explanations, not as a judgment about the player.

The decision assessment uses the expected conditions available when the month began. The outcome comparison shows what actually occurred. Keep the initial forecast intact when reviewing a month: replacing it with hindsight would make forecasting skill impossible to assess.

The headline is a **0–100 decision score** with a simple letter: **A ≥ 90, B ≥ 80, C ≥ 70, D ≥ 60, F < 60**. The score combines these dimensions:

| Dimension | Maximum points |
|---|---:|
| Earnings discipline | 25 |
| Cash and inventory discipline | 20 |
| Serving customers | 20 |
| Quality and future capability | 20 |
| Liquidity resilience | 15 |

Within each dimension, 35% reflects a modeled operating standard and 65% reflects the gap to the strongest dimension result among tested feasible policies. These are teaching priorities, not external benchmarks of executive competence. Borrowing alone does not create earnings or total liquidity resources; the latter includes both cash and unused corporate credit. The final campaign board score remains a separate measure of the whole run.

The hindsight comparison asks how alternative plans would have performed from the same opening position under the same realized conditions. **Best tested** means the strongest result among the plans the coach evaluated under its stated score. It is not a proof of a global optimum, advice to copy those decisions into a different month, or the best possible twelve-month strategy. Different goals and longer investment horizons can favor a different plan.

Open **What worked, what to change** after a month, or **Results → Coach** beside a past month. Inspect an alternative's actual decision changes as well as its expected and realized results. Historical comparisons require the stored campaign to reproduce the recorded results; if a modified or unsupported history cannot be replayed, coaching reports that limitation.

Useful questions after a round are: Did price cover the relevant costs? Did output match the dealer need? Was cash protected? Did quality and service hold up? Did a choice improve this month's score by sacrificing something valuable later? Did the market move, or did our position within it move?

## Understand the cycle and the surprise

The public market outlook describes a scenario's expected direction. Seasonality describes the pattern within the year. Realized shocks can move the actual month away from that expectation. These are distinct: an upswing can have a weak month, and a downcycle can have a seasonal rebound.

New cyclical campaigns start with a **Soft market**, **Flat market**, or **Upswing market** outlook. The forecast updates as observed conditions change; demand turns can persist across months, and supplier disruptions can take time to recover. A saved seed permits a reproducible campaign: the same model, opening state, seed, and decisions produce the same results. Reproducibility makes comparisons fair; changing the scenario seed creates a different challenge. Expected forecasts do not reveal future surprise outcomes.

Existing campaigns retain their original market rules. Start a new campaign to use the cyclical model, and export a backup first if you want to retain the previous run. The [market-cycle model](market-cycles.md) documents the scenario and replay mechanics.

The game uses this structure to practice adjustment, not to teach that every downturn lasts a fixed number of months. Preserve options, watch channel inventory, and revisit assumptions after each result. Do not treat the annual outlook as a guarantee.

## Historical equipment-industry context

These dated examples explain why cycles and channel inventory belong in the game. They are not a statement about today's market:

- CNH reported **$24.7 billion of consolidated revenue in 2023, up 5%** from 2022. Its 2024 annual report records **$19.836 billion in 2024, down 19.7%**; Agriculture net sales fell **22.8%** to $14.007 billion. Company revenue can change sharply between years, and it is not the same measure as industry retail units. [CNH's 2023 results](https://media.cnh.com/emea/cnh/cnh-industrial-n.v.-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2023-results/s/69bb93ad-675a-48e1-9eff-dce6c9c4d030) and [2024 Form 10-K, pp. 50–51](https://www.cnh.com/-/media/CNHi/cnhicorporate/Investor-relation/financial_information/annual_reports/2024/CNH-2024-10-K.pdf?rev=-1).
- CNH said Agriculture dealer inventory fell by **over $700 million in Q4 2024**, accompanied by **34% fewer production hours**. Cutting factory output can be a deliberate response to excess channel stock. [CNH's 2024 results, released February 4, 2025](https://media.cnh.com/ASIA-PACIFIC-ENGLISH/cnh/cnh-industrial-n.v.-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results/s/f5b51dd6-aa53-4d1c-bcbf-6db7ca015350).
- In 2024, CNH reported North American industry unit volumes falling **16% for tractors over 140 hp** and **22% for combines**. Asia Pacific combine demand rose **11%**. A weak aggregate market can contain different regional and product conditions. The annual report also identifies farm income, commodity prices, financing, and weather as demand influences. [2024 Form 10-K, pp. 5–7, 46 and 50–51](https://www.cnh.com/-/media/CNHi/cnhicorporate/Investor-relation/financial_information/annual_reports/2024/CNH-2024-10-K.pdf?rev=-1).

The first practice goal is to explain a decision before making it, then explain the difference between the forecast and the result afterward. Better business judgment comes from understanding that difference across several rounds.
