# Development roadmap

The next versions should deepen the decisions already available: **What can the market absorb? What will this plan earn? What is limiting delivery? Which investment helps, and can the company fund it?** More scenery should support those decisions, while the business model remains inspectable.

The current game already provides monthly market and share estimates, balance-sheet projections, unit economics, historical coaching, cyclical market scenarios, [factory and cost diagnostics](factory-diagnostics.md), [market-position history and perception factors](market-position.md), [investment comparisons](investments.md), [fixed board mandates](board-mandate.md), [customer-financing explanations](financing-guide.md), and continuous [three-year campaigns](multi-year.md). The investment lab tests the existing shared training, quality, maintenance, capacity, and engineering budgets under the public expected outlook. The monthly report includes the full balance sheet with account movements, and board reviews compare actual results with disclosed monthly goals and quarterly milestones. Coaching separately evaluates decision quality under the original public forecast. The game does not yet model individual employees, detailed equipment projects, autonomous rivals, or a market valuation of the company.

The executive release adds six monthly colleague consultations, fictional rival benchmarks with demand effects, paid supplier and dealer support, six-month best-tested strategy comparisons, and easy/medium/difficult employment rules. These are documented in the [executive campaign rules](executive-campaign.md). Colleagues are explanatory roles, not an employee staffing simulation; rivals are observable benchmarks, not autonomous businesses.

The priorities below are proposed work, not implemented features. All market sizes, competitors, facilities, and financial terms would remain synthetic gameplay assumptions unless a separately documented calibration project establishes otherwise. This is not a forecast of CNH's business.

| Priority | Player benefit | Model and interface prerequisites | Completion test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Player budgets and variance bridges | Set a financial budget alongside the board mandate, then explain divergences and decide what to change. | Preserve a player-approved baseline budget separately from the editable forecast and fixed board targets. Use product-level volume, price, costs, and working capital to explain differences without rewriting that baseline. | A monthly review reconciles revenue, profit, and cash differences to understandable causes; changing the next plan leaves past budgets intact. |
| 2. Scenario stress tests | Understand how much cash protection is needed if demand falls or supply deteriorates. | Explicit user-selected demand, supplier, and funding assumptions applied consistently across the same multi-month projection. Keep scenarios separate from committed state and show the month of minimum cash. | The player can compare a base case with a named stress case, identify the cash gap, and test an operational or funding response. A scenario is not presented as a statistical confidence interval. |
| 3. Regional markets and competitors | Choose where and how to compete instead of relying on a single aggregate market. | Stable market boundaries, regional demand, dealer reach, transport costs, and rival price/capacity policies. Retail sales must reconcile with regional market totals; competitor decisions must have visible rules. | Price, product, and delivery decisions change regional share for explainable reasons. Growing industry demand is distinguishable from winning customers from rivals. |
| 4. Detailed labor, capacity, and supplier systems | Extend current flow diagnostics into staffing and recovery decisions at specific work areas. | Add shifts, headcount, a cross-training matrix, hiring/training delays, station capacity, component lead times, and selectable supplier recovery projects. Current workforce capability is only a shared skill proxy; preserve links between inspections and engine quantities. | The player can trace an unfilled order to a station, skill, or supplier constraint and compare the cost and timing of overtime, staffing, inventory, or supplier development. |
| 5. Facility projects and capital allocation | Use the existing multi-year horizon to choose between specific expansion, modernization, and product-development projects. | Add facility-specific economics, project milestones, commissioning/ramp-up delays, and explicit alternatives to the current shared capex and engineering budgets. Preserve continuous obligations and assets across years. | The player can trace a project's cash commitments, usable capability, and incremental results against a comparable no-project plan. Unfinished or idle capacity is not presented as an earned return. |
| 6. Financing terms and covenants | Run the manufacturer and customer-finance business with an understandable funding strategy. | Explicit maturities, rates, repayments, covenant definitions, and asset/liability classification; keep corporate facilities separate from financing-book funding. Add loan-cohort credit and residual-value assumptions before introducing more complex products. | Borrowing improves liquidity while creating a visible future obligation. The player sees covenant headroom, refinancing dates, and customer-credit exposure before a breach or funding shortfall. |

## Release approach

Keep a complete monthly loop in every release: plan, project, commit, explain, and revise. Add a new controllable system only when its consequences can be reconciled in the operating report and financial statements. Preserve existing campaigns when adding read-only analysis; version saves explicitly when new state changes the operating rules.

Playtests should begin with concrete operator tasks: identify the coming demand change, distinguish sales growth from share growth, reconcile a tractor's cost and inventory value, identify the binding factory constraint, compare an investment with keeping the working plan while protecting cash and delivery, explain the difference between customer APR and company funding costs, and identify which published board target needs attention. Multi-year playtests should follow projects and debt across December without resetting expectations or balances. Mobile readability and desktop 3D inspection both matter, but access to financial decisions must not depend on navigating the scene. Numerical tests establish consistency; browser and player testing establish whether the game is understandable and enjoyable.
