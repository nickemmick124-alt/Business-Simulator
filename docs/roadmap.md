# Development roadmap

The next versions should help the player answer three questions before committing: **What can the market absorb? What will this plan earn? Can the company fund it?** More scenery should support those decisions, while the business model remains inspectable.

The priorities below are proposed work, not implemented features. All market sizes, competitors, facilities, and financial terms would remain synthetic gameplay assumptions unless a separately documented calibration project establishes otherwise. This is not a forecast of CNH's business.

| Priority | Player benefit | Model and interface prerequisites | Completion test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Budget versus actual | Set a target, see where performance diverged, and decide what to change. | Preserve a baseline budget separately from the editable forecast. Record product-level volume, price, costs, and working capital so the review can explain differences without rewriting the original target. | A monthly review reconciles the revenue, profit, and cash differences to understandable causes; changing the next plan leaves past budgets intact. |
| 2. Scenario stress tests | Understand how much cash protection is needed if demand falls or supply deteriorates. | Explicit user-selected demand, supplier, and funding assumptions applied consistently across the same multi-month projection. Keep scenarios separate from committed state and show the month of minimum cash. | The player can compare a base case with a named stress case, identify the cash gap, and test an operational or funding response. A scenario is not presented as a statistical confidence interval. |
| 3. Regional markets and competitors | Choose where and how to compete instead of relying on a single aggregate market. | Stable market boundaries, regional demand, dealer reach, transport costs, and rival price/capacity policies. Retail sales must reconcile with regional market totals; competitor decisions must have visible rules. | Price, product, and delivery decisions change regional share for explainable reasons. Growing industry demand is distinguishable from winning customers from rivals. |
| 4. Labor, capacity, and supplier lead times | Diagnose why a factory misses its plan and choose the right investment or recovery action. | Separate shifts, headcount, hiring/training delays, bottleneck capacity, component lead times, and supplier recovery projects. Link factory inspection to the same quantities and constraints used by the engine. | The player can trace an unfilled order to a specific limiting resource and compare the cost and timing of overtime, staffing, inventory, or supplier development. |
| 5. Multi-year facilities and capital allocation | Weigh expansion, modernization, and product development over a useful investment horizon. | Extend the campaign beyond one year; introduce facility-specific economics, project milestones, ramp-up delays, depreciation, and persistent demand assumptions. Carry obligations and asset value through year boundaries. | A project consumes cash during construction, creates usable capacity after commissioning, and earns its return through subsequent sales. The score does not reward unfinished or idle capacity as if it were productive. |
| 6. Financing terms and covenants | Run the manufacturer and customer-finance business with an understandable funding strategy. | Explicit maturities, rates, repayments, covenant definitions, and asset/liability classification; keep corporate facilities separate from financing-book funding. Add loan-cohort credit and residual-value assumptions before introducing more complex products. | Borrowing improves liquidity while creating a visible future obligation. The player sees covenant headroom, refinancing dates, and customer-credit exposure before a breach or funding shortfall. |

## Release approach

Keep a complete monthly loop in every release: plan, project, commit, explain, and revise. Add a new controllable system only when its consequences can be reconciled in the operating report and financial statements. Preserve existing campaigns when adding read-only analysis; version saves explicitly when new state changes the operating rules.

Playtests should begin with concrete operator tasks: identify the coming demand change, estimate the attainable share, spot excess inventory, and protect cash without sacrificing delivery. Mobile readability and desktop 3D inspection both matter, but access to financial decisions must not depend on navigating the scene. Numerical tests establish consistency; browser and player testing establish whether the game is understandable and enjoyable.
